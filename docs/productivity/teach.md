# teach

> Build understanding through practice.

## What it does

`teach` helps a learner make sense of a concept and use it, letting their attempts reveal what to explain next. A longer explanation is not automatically a better one; the goal is an explanation suited to their starting point plus a way to try the idea, with understanding demonstrated by reasoning or action rather than agreement alone.

It starts by inferring the learner's goal and prior knowledge from their question, asking a targeted question or giving a small prediction task when the starting point is uncertain — without turning a clear request into an intake interview. Trouble is diagnosed into one of three kinds, because they need different repairs: unfamiliar vocabulary, a missing prerequisite, or a mistaken mental model. The teaching itself picks a capability just beyond what the learner can already do, starts from a concrete situation they recognize, works through what happens and why, and only then names the general rule. New terms are defined before they're depended on, and analogies come with a mark showing where they stop matching the subject.

The learner predicts an outcome, explains a contrast, traces a process or tries a nearby case; feedback targets their reasoning, with a hint before taking over. A struggling attempt triggers teaching the missing foundation; a successful one reduces support or varies the case. When an explanation doesn't land, the entry point or representation changes: plainer language, missing context supplied, a replaced analogy — the claim stays intact rather than the same abstraction arriving with more emphasis, and a different small task checks the new version. A bundled reference covers designing exercises and continuing across sessions.

## When to reach for it

Say "teach me X", "explain X to me", or "help me understand why this works".

## It's working if

- Understanding shows up in the learner's reasoning or actions, not in them agreeing.
- Each unfamiliar term is defined before the explanation depends on it.
- Attempts drive the pace: struggles trigger foundation repair, successes reduce support or vary the case.
- Failed explanations get a new entry point or representation, not the same one repeated louder.
- Consequential or disputed claims are checked against trustworthy sources, and teaching simplifications are labeled as such.

## Common questions

**Will it just give me the answer?**
It gives the explanation the capability needs and feedback on attempts, with hints before takeovers. Teaching aims at understanding, not compliance with a preferred answer.

**What about graded or assessed work?**
If the learner identifies graded assessment work, it teaches the method, offers hints or uses an analogous exercise instead of completing the submission.

**How big does a lesson get?**
Matched to the request. A quick question does not require a course or a learning workspace, and persistent learning artifacts are created only when asked for.

**What if the analogy breaks down?**
The explanation marks where the analogy stops matching the subject. If an explanation fails to land, the analogy may be replaced entirely while the underlying claim stays intact.

## Where it fits

In the productivity set, `teach` is the understanding stop: [research](research.md) fetches the facts a lesson verifies against, and [writing](writing.md) shapes any lasting document that comes out of it. It differs from [next](next.md), which recommends a step rather than building skill, and from [idea-development](../design/idea-development.md), which makes decisions instead of explaining them.
