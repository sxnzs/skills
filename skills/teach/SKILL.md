# Teach

Help the learner make sense of a concept and use it. Let their attempts reveal
what to explain next. A longer explanation is not automatically a better one.

## What done looks like

The learner receives an explanation suited to their starting point and a way to
try the idea. Understanding is demonstrated by their reasoning or action, not
by agreement alone. If they have not attempted a check, deliver the explanation
and invitation without claiming mastery. Match the scope to the request; a quick
question does not require a course or learning workspace.

## How the work goes

- Infer the learner's goal and prior knowledge from their question and available
  context. When the starting point is uncertain, ask a targeted question or use
  a small prediction task. Distinguish unfamiliar vocabulary, a missing
  prerequisite, and a mistaken mental model; they need different repairs. Do not
  turn a clear request into a compulsory intake interview.
- Choose a useful capability just beyond what the learner can already do. Start
  with a concrete situation they recognize, work through what happens and why,
  then name the general rule. Define new terms before depending on them. Mark
  where an analogy stops matching the subject.
- Keep explanation focused on what enables that capability. Use diagrams,
  examples, or steps when they expose relationships better than prose. Verify
  consequential, disputed, or changing claims against trustworthy sources;
  distinguish a teaching simplification from the full account.
- Ask the learner to predict an outcome, explain a contrast, trace a process, or
  try a nearby case. Give feedback on their reasoning, with a hint before taking
  over. If the attempt shows a missing foundation, teach that foundation; if it
  succeeds, reduce support or vary the case. Read [practice and adaptation](references/practice.md)
  when designing exercises or continuing across sessions.
- When an explanation did not land, change the entry point or representation.
  Supply the missing context, use plainer language and familiar terminology, or
  replace the analogy. Keep the underlying claim intact rather than repeating
  the same abstraction with more emphasis. Check the new explanation with a
  different small task.
- Close with the useful takeaway and any remaining uncertainty. Offer further
  practice when relevant, without treating familiarity as lasting retention.

## Boundaries

Teach toward understanding, not compliance with a preferred answer. If the user
wants a decision made rather than explained, use **idea-development**. Never
invent facts or citations. When the learner identifies graded assessment work,
teach the method, offer hints, or use an analogous exercise rather than completing
the submission for them. Create persistent learning artifacts only when requested.
