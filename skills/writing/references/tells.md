# Tells checklist

Read when a draft sounds formulaic or the request is to remove AI-writing tells.
These are editing cues, not evidence of who wrote a passage. Judge patterns in
context; a punctuation mark or formal word alone warrants no rewrite.

- Replace inflated significance and sales language with the actual supported
  claim. Remove decorative explanations that merely announce importance.
- Inspect vague appeals to experts and speculative gap-filling. Keep genuine
  uncertainty; name an available source or flag missing support instead of
  manufacturing authority or detail.
- Prefer direct verbs and visible actors where they clarify responsibility.
  Remove ceremonial introductions, inflated substitutes for simple verbs, and
  repeated qualifiers that obscure the degree of certainty.
- Keep one name for one concept. Synonym rotation can make the reader mistake a
  repeated idea for a new one. Check whether apparent ranges describe a real
  continuum and whether list items are genuinely parallel.
- Break repeated rhetorical templates: forced triples, staged contrasts,
  dramatic fragments, fake intimacy, and aphorisms without a concrete claim.
  Preserve deliberate emphasis and the author's characteristic cadence.
- Cut contrasts with an alternative nobody raised ("this is X, not Y"), invented
  compound labels that name no real thing, and statements of what you will not
  do. Remove formulaic closing lines ("In short", "Bottom line") and stock
  wording (delve, leverage, foster, genuinely, importantly) when they add no
  meaning; keep requested summaries and precise terminology.
- Remove headings that promise nothing, paragraphs that restate their heading,
  automatic upbeat endings, and chat pleasantries pasted into an article.
  Keep useful navigation and genre-appropriate greetings.
- Let formatting serve comprehension. Reconsider decorative bold, emojis, and
  repeated inline labels; retain punctuation and capitalization that fit the
  author's sample or the publication's style.
- Describe the current system in durable documentation. Describe changes when
  the genre is versioned, as in release notes or migration guidance.

After editing, read aloud for rhythm and compare against the source for meaning.
Preserve distinctive details, mixed feelings, and genuine asides. Neutral technical
prose needs clarity, not an injected personality. Never add a fact, quotation, or
opinion merely to make the rewrite sound more human.
